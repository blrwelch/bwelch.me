Dear ngrok Hiring Team,

The way ngrok describes this role — "translating technical depth into approachable experiences, building for users who notice every detail, finding clarity in areas without established playbooks" — is the closest I've seen any job description come to articulating what I actually do.

I've spent 17 years designing interfaces for technically demanding contexts. At C Spire, I was a UX Developer: I designed the flows and wrote the HTML, CSS, and JavaScript that shipped them. Three full flagship site relaunches — wireless, business services, careers — all responsive, all production-ready, handling the kinds of complex flows (upgrade eligibility, device financing, multi-step account management) that break when you don't understand the system underneath. Working in code made me a better designer. It made handoffs cleaner, edge cases easier to catch, and gave me the fluency to know what's actually hard versus what just sounds hard.

At AutoZone, I led product discovery design for one of the highest-traffic retail e-commerce platforms in the US. The users were often people who knew more about the domain than I ever would — experienced mechanics, fleet managers, parts specialists — people who notice when a filter is scoped wrong or a result is missing. That audience sharpened my instinct for designing with precision rather than assumption. At Lextech, I've spent the last four years doing the same thing ngrok does, just at the application layer: taking technically dense systems — AI agent frameworks, workflow automation, healthcare field tools — and turning them into something the humans using them can trust and act on.

I use AI tools throughout my work — Claude and ChatGPT for synthesis and ideation, Cursor for prototyping and front-end iteration. I'm not using AI to replace judgment; I'm using it to compress the distance between question and answer so I can spend more time on the parts that actually require a designer.

What draws me to ngrok specifically is the nature of the translation problem. You're building for developers — people who are sharp, skeptical, and will immediately feel it if the design doesn't reflect an understanding of their world. I have the technical background and the product instincts to meet them there. I've also spent most of my career on small teams with high ownership, where the work is still being figured out and where shipping something real matters more than shipping something perfect.

My portfolio is at bwelch.me. The AI insights dashboard and healthcare iPad case studies are good starting points for the kind of technically complex, high-clarity work that's most relevant to what ngrok is doing. I'd love to talk.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Brittany Welch
